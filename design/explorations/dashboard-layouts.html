Exploration · 09 Gamer dashboard

Dashboard layouts

Four ways to lay out the same dashboard, as rough wireframes. Each one holds exactly the busy account's content (Mon 5 Oct, 14:12 SGT): four things to do, two sessions coming up, one reported problem and one recent session. None of them uses side-by-side columns of uneven cards. Colours and sizes here are only there to tell parts apart; the chosen layout gets the real components.

Option A

One column, in order of urgency

Recommended

Every section stacked in one column of fixed width: To do, Coming up, Problems you've reported, Recent sessions. Every card has the same anatomy in the same places: status on top, title, the booking line, the deadline on the right, actions at the bottom. You read it top to bottom like a to-do list.

  • Clearest order: the most urgent thing is always first, then the next
  • Longest page; Coming up sits below the To do cards on a busy day
  • Phone and desktop read the same way, so nothing moves between widths
  • Wide screens leave space on the right (kept for readable line lengths)
  • Keeps every piece of content and every action as it is today

Option B

Compact rows, like an inbox

Each section is a list of one-line rows with fixed columns: status, what's needed, the booking, the time left and the action, so everything lines up down the page. The row that needs you first opens out to show its details (the offered times, the confirm text); the others show their details when you open them.

  • Most scannable: the whole dashboard fits on one screen on desktop
  • Some details (offered times, explanations) hide until you open a row
  • Columns line up, so deadlines and actions are always in the same place
  • Rows are tight for two buttons; on phone each row stacks to two lines
  • Reuses the session list's look, so 09 and 10 feel like one family

Option C

Agenda by day

One timeline, grouped by day: Today, Tomorrow, then each date, then Later. Deadlines (pay by, confirm by, reply by) and sessions sit together at their time, so the page answers "what happens when". Problems and recent sessions follow under the timeline.

  • Reads like a calendar: the order is simply time
  • Things to do and sessions mix in one list, so "what needs me" is less obvious
  • Shows how close things are to each other (a deadline just before a session)
  • A review due in three weeks sits far down, under Later
  • Good for gamers with many sessions a week

Option D

One focus card, then a single list

The one thing that matters most right now gets a full-width card at the top (here, finishing the payment; at session time, Join session). Below it, everything else is one ordered list of plain rows, grouped under small headings, with one action per row.

  • Strongest focus: one obvious next step, then a tidy list
  • The other to-dos get less room than in A (one action each)
  • Calm page with one card, so it never looks crowded
  • Which thing is "the focus" changes, so the top of the page changes often
  • The focus card is also where Join session appears when a session is about to start
